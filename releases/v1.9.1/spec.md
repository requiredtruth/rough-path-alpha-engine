## Rough Path indicator
The turquoise line and signal strip overlay the selected market. Parameters are persisted per app: rolling sample window, score threshold, efficiency gate, signature area weight, noise passthrough gain, band volatility window, band multiplier, projection slope window, horizon and score weight. Defaults: window 32, threshold 0.8, efficiency 0.1, area weight 1, gain 0.18, band window 32, band multiplier 1.5, projection window 16, base horizon 12 samples, rendered/evaluated horizon 36 samples, projection weight 0.5.

For each rolling time/log-price path, level-2 signatures are computed with Chen concatenation for piecewise linear increments. For a segment increment d, S2 += S1 tensor d + 0.5 d tensor d, then S1 += d. The antisymmetric area is (S12−S21)/2. Time is normalized within the window. Prefix sums compute this rolling area in O(1) per sample. A lead–lag lift's area magnitude equals half the summed squared log-price increments. This quadratic variation normalizes directional return and area; efficiency equals absolute net log return divided by total absolute log variation.

Raw score = log return / sqrt(quadratic variation) + areaWeight * area / sqrt(quadratic variation). The structural score scales by (0.5 + 0.5 * smooth), where the legacy `smooth` parameter now controls noise passthrough rather than EMA smoothing. The price center is a causal geometric play operator: tube = close * sqrt(QV / window) * (1 - efficiency) * (1 - smooth); center = clamp(previousCenter, close - tube, close + tube). Straight directional paths pass without EMA phase lag; reversals inside the tube can still have threshold response delay. A truncated signature alone does not remove all microstructural noise, and time-augmented paths are not globally invariant under arbitrary time reparameterization.

Signals require ready windows, the efficiency gate, score threshold, and a matching price residual against the geometric center (nonnegative for long, nonpositive for short). The adjusted score adds projectionWeight times a bounded slope estimate normalized by base channel half-width. This replaces the former lag-driven border-breakout rule. Old optimization reports are displayable but cannot be applied until rerun with engine 1.6.0. Gap fills are excluded; gaps reset warmup. Historical optimizations have no live skew observations and cannot reconstruct historical OI/CVD/funding.

### Upper/lower borders and dotted projection
Gold upper and rose lower borders enclose the turquoise causal center. Channel half-width = bandMult * center * max(1e-6, sqrt(sum of squared log returns over bandWindow)). A faint fill helps distinguish the channel from trade/liquidation lines. Channel warmup and gap resets suppress signals until all required windows are ready.

Each dotted line starts at the exact current value of its own solid counterpart. Its own least-squares slope is fitted over projectionWindow recent samples; when that slope opposes the last segment's direction, the last segment slope is used so the continuation does not immediately point the wrong way. The projected horizon is three times projectionSteps: a base value of 12 renders 36 future samples. The center and both borders have separate trajectories. Borders are bounded against the center if extrapolated lines would cross, and prices stay positive. These are visual extrapolations, not real future prices or guaranteed confidence bounds.

Projection score contribution uses the same direction-adjusted center slope over the triple horizon: projectionWeight * clamp(slope * (3 * projectionSteps) / current half-width, -5, 5). Thus the searched base horizon participates in the actual strategy. A zero projection weight remains an available baseline.

The chart computes the overlay on the selected candle timeframe, shown in the chart details and signal strip. Its lookbacks and horizon count these chart samples. Research uses the selected historical dataset's samples (one-minute candles or ticks), so parameters tested on one-minute data correspond directly to the one-minute chart. On larger chart timeframes the same algorithm is recalculated at that resolution. Projections appear only at the latest edge, with no hindsight projection while panning old data. The chart reserves future space dynamically so the extended forecast is visible. The price scale includes visible channel and projection bounds. Center, borders/fill and dotted projection visibility are independently adjustable. This is an original channel presentation; no proprietary LuxAlgo source or formula is used.

## Historical data
Research screen: Rough α button near Stats. Select UTC from/until dates, exclusive until. Last year defaults to 365 completed days, last week to seven. Coinbase Exchange BTC-USD one-minute OHLCV uses 200-bucket pages below the 300-bucket API limit. Typical year: approximately 525,600 samples and 2,628 pages. Leap-year full-calendar data can have 527,040 samples. One book supports 600,000 minute buckets; split larger studies into separate date ranges. The app downloads data itself; no year-sized dataset is bundled.

Each page is normalized, sorted, deduplicated and committed to IndexedDB before checkpoint progress advances. Pausing or interruption leaves committed pages reusable. HTTP transient failures retry with exponential backoff; permanent HTTP 400/401/403/404 fails visibly. Empty or incomplete candle windows are counted as unavailable rather than filled. Retry missing candles deletes sparse cached pages and refetches them. Interrupted jobs resume by pressing Download / resume with the same dates. UI downloads and workers run while the app remains active; Android may suspend or kill research work in the background. Reopen and resume cached downloads. Completed results and the last valid data range, grid and cost settings persist.

Actual Coinbase trade ticks use `/products/BTC-USD/trades?limit=1000&after=...` with the oldest trade ID as the pagination cursor. Pages and cursor are checkpointed separately in the same cache. Tick IDs deduplicate samples; equal timestamps retain deterministic trade ordering. Downloads stop after crossing the requested start. API errors, archive exhaustion or non-advancing cursors remain visible; partial data is not labeled complete. Since this API walks backward from the latest trades, older dates can require substantial download time and storage. Tick optimization is capped at 1.1 million samples per book. A year of candles and a year of ticks have very different resource requirements; choose shorter tick books. Candle data is always labeled candles, not ticks.

JSON OHLCV import: array of `{t,o,h,l,c,v}`, timestamp in milliseconds, sorted unique timestamps, valid positive OHLC, rows within the selected date interval, max 32 MB per imported file. Android system file picker is used. No synthetic historical prices are inserted.

## On-device optimizer and simulator
Dedicated Web Worker keeps the terminal responsive. Packed transferable Float64 sample buffers avoid cloning all candle objects into the worker. Rolling features are reused across candidates sharing a window. Grids accept up to 20 comma-separated values per dimension and at most 512 candidates. Default grid has 256 candidates. Pause/cancel terminates optimization immediately; completed reports are retained.

Chronological splits: first 60% training, next 20% validation, final 20% holdout. Each split begins with fresh simulation cash and no position. Prior samples may warm up features, but no positions carry across splits. Candidates are ranked by min(training ROI − 1.5 * drawdown, validation ROI − 1.5 * drawdown), with at least five trades in both selection splits. Final holdout is evaluated only after selecting the best candidate. Repeated human tuning after inspecting holdout invalidates its status as untouched.

Signals from sample i act at sample i+1 open. Position size is an editable percentage of current cash, with one position at a time; neutral or reversed signal closes the position. Leverage ranges from 1x to 500x. Editable opening/closing fee and adverse slippage apply to notional, plus time-based borrowing and signed funding. Intracandle high/low triggers the estimated liquidation threshold, with adverse gap-open handling. Position loss is capped at collateral. Entry costs that already breach liquidation are skipped. Missing minute gaps above 90 seconds or tick gaps above five minutes close positions at the last available price and reset indicator features. This explicitly assumes liquidation at last quote for gap separation, not knowledge of prices within the gap. OHLC cannot reconstruct the complete ordering of intraminute prices.

Report: ROI, max drawdown, trades, win rate, fees, holding cost, profit factor, candidate parameters, selection score, chronological date splits, gaps, skipped costly entries and holdout equity curve. Export JSON to Downloads. By default, the best eligible current-engine parameters are applied to the live chart after a search. This can be disabled; Apply selected indicator copies them manually. Prior-engine reports remain visible but must be rerun before applying to the new channel strategy; it does not place orders. Automatic optimization after download is enabled by default and can be disabled. The optimizer searches all ten indicator dimensions plus leverage (1–500x) and collateral allocation (>0–100% of current equity). Lists and inclusive `min:max:step` ranges define finite domains; each axis supports up to 10,001 values. The Cartesian product is counted exactly with BigInt and decoded lazily, never materialized in memory. The old 512-candidate optimizer limit is removed. A run evaluates 1–1,000,000 unique candidates, clamped to the available space.

Methods: **grid** visits every combination in deterministic order when the budget covers the space; **random** uses seeded uniform proposals without duplicate evaluations; **simulated annealing** changes neighboring parameter indices, accepts worse scores with `exp(delta/(temperature * scoreScale))`, cools geometrically from 1 to 0.01 over 250 proposals, and restarts from the best candidate every 250 proposals; **hybrid** allocates approximately 20% of the budget to evenly spaced grid exploration, 40% to random sampling, and the remainder to annealing. Duplicate proposals fall back to random exploration and eventually unvisited grid entries, guaranteeing unique evaluations even near exhaustion. The seed makes proposals reproducible for unchanged data and settings. No method guarantees the global optimum on an incompletely covered space.

The Huge search preset defines broad indicator ranges, every integer leverage from 1 through 500, and every integer collateral percentage from 1 through 100, with a 100,000-candidate hybrid budget. This is sampled coverage of the displayed much larger domain. Budget for entire space selects exhaustive grid mode only for spaces with at most 1,000,000 combinations; narrow ranges to exhaust larger spaces. Continuous real-valued possibilities cannot all be enumerated: selected step sizes define coverage. Fees, slippage, funding, borrowing, liquidation threshold and initial balance remain explicit fixed assumptions rather than optimizable market costs.

Candidate-specific leverage and collateral are used on training, validation and the final selected holdout. Indicator signals/features are reused for adjacent identical settings, with a bounded one-entry cache. Only ten leaders and visited candidate identifiers are retained. Reports record selected risk, method, seed, finite domain, unique count, full/sample coverage, elapsed time, annealing acceptance/restarts and numerical rejection count. Candidates with nonfinite equity or compounded equity exceeding 1e100 are rejected without terminating the other searches; a numerically invalid holdout is labeled unevaluable. Selected risk settings do not alter live order controls. Indicator parameters alone can be auto-applied.

Progress displays method, coverage, best risk, train/validation ROI, elapsed time and estimated remaining time. Huge annual-data studies may require hours or days; cost grows with dataset size times evaluated candidates. Keep the app active. The worker isolates computation from chart rendering but does not survive Android process termination. Cancel discards an unfinished search; downloaded history remains cached. The app does not label sampled exploration as exhaustive.

## Full-spectrum live OI / spot / funding skew (1.6.0)
The gold/rose borders and matching dotted projections use combined Arbitrum (42161), Polygon (137), and Base (8453) exposure. Each chain has an independent open-trades snapshot and collateral/pair registry. Market identities are resolved by symbol using that chain's own pair indices. Regular and DEGEN variants are combined by underlying asset. For every collateral with verified metadata, position notional = raw collateral amount / collateral precision * raw leverage / 1000 * current collateral USD price. All collateral types are eligible; absent USD prices are excluded and counted as partial coverage. No token amount is assumed to equal USD. Identical user/index values on different chains remain distinct; duplicates within one chain are removed.

Long OI accumulation uses snapshots at least one minute apart. Previous collateral-unit exposure is revalued at current collateral USD prices before computing growth, so a rising ETH collateral price alone cannot masquerade as new long positioning. Chain/registry coverage changes reset comparability. Per-chain long/short USD exposure, unpriced-position counts, and availability are shown in the Rough α coverage table; the main chart identifies participating chains. Funding is fetched for every contributing chain/market/collateral group and weighted by its long USD notional. A missing contributing funding rate disables skew rather than silently becoming zero or borrowing another chain's rate. Polygon execution fee settings remain separate from these indicator-only multi-chain rates.

Spot CVD uses continuous Coinbase Exchange matches + heartbeats and Kraken v2 trade streams for the selected USD crypto asset, with automatic reconnection. Coinbase side is the maker side (sell means taker buy); Kraken side is the taker side (buy means taker buy). Flow is signed USD trade notional over a two-minute window. Per-venue/book trade IDs suppress duplicates and detect gaps; reconnects, heartbeat gaps or missing IDs reset a two-minute warmup. Second buckets bound accumulation memory, rather than sampling the latest 100 trades or retaining every print. Only warmed, fresh venues contribute. Venue coverage is displayed (Coinbase/Kraken, up to 2/2); this is coverage of those venues, not a claim to cover every exchange worldwide. Unsupported spot pairs, closed/throttled sockets, missing rates and offline chains are explicitly unavailable/partial. Foreground streams resume and warm up after suspension; historical or background spot flow is never fabricated.

Let clip clamp to [0,1]. Weakness = clip(-signedSpotNotional / totalSpotNotional); crowding = clip((longOI-shortOI)/(longOI+shortOI)); growth = clip(longOIGrowthFraction/0.02); fundingStrength = clip(longFundingDecimalPerHour/0.00001). Pressure = weakness * crowding * growth * (0.5 + 0.5*fundingStrength). Upper = center + baseWidth*(1-0.8*pressure); lower = max(1e-9, center-baseWidth*(1+0.5*pressure)). This compresses upside and expands downside when longs accumulate against weak spot flow. Pressure is a heuristic, not a liquidation probability. The geometric center is unchanged by skew.

Observation components expire after 120 seconds; spot stream activity must be recent within 15 seconds. Missing, nonfinite, future or invalid inputs result in neutral symmetric bands. Observations are bounded session memory, joined as-of candle end (or current time for the open candle), and never backfilled onto earlier candles. The historical optimizer has no invented OI, funding or spot-flow history. Full configured coverage means 3 fresh, fully priced chains and 2 warmed spot venues with funding for all contributing long exposure. Partial operation remains labeled rather than described as complete.

## Network resilience (1.4.0)
Read-only Gains HTTPS requests and price/public WebSockets have service-specific gains.trade → gains.dev fallback, including DNS, TLS, socket, timeout, HTTP and invalid-JSON failures. Native Android background prices/history and foreground charts, holdings, registry, multi-chain public trades and candle history are covered. Service paths, chain IDs and queries are preserved; HTTPS/WSS and certificate verification stay enabled. A blocked secondary remains unavailable and retries; the app never treats a hostname alone as proof of health. Read failures never resubmit signed orders.

Primary health is retried after 60 seconds. Foreground WebSockets perform make-before-break recovery: a primary probe must deliver a valid application frame (fresh prices for pricing) before replacing the working secondary. Native background monitoring uses the same fresh accepted-price requirement. Connect/first-frame and stale-stream deadlines prevent stuck sockets. HTTP and WebSocket circuits are independent. Exact service hosts are allowlisted by suffix, excluding lookalike domains. Read-only GitHub-runner audits on 2026-10-16 found legacy `.trade` transport resets, nonexistent `.dev` pricing subdomains, and working `api.gains.dev/v1/arbitrum` REST/WSS services. The corresponding Polygon/Base API routes returned 404; their separate legacy chain backends remained available on `.dev`. Domain availability is an observation, not a claim about ownership or future availability.

## v1.8.6 — Arrow paper bot

The **Bot** button beside **Close** in Positions starts paper trading the selected market's Rough Path arrows. **Stop bot** disables new automatic entries; existing positions remain yours to inspect or close. Starting selects PAPER and Positions. Bot trades share the normal paper wallet (initially $100; use Respawn to reset), positions, P&L, history and entry/liquidation chart lines. They carry a BOT label.

Each newly confirmed green arrow adds a long; each red arrow adds a short. Before opening on the opposite arrow, all opposite-side **bot-owned positions in that execution market** close at the current fresh mark and their net proceeds return to paper cash. Manual positions remain untouched. A neutral interval does not close anything. Another same-side arrow after a neutral interval adds another position. A persistent same-side signal is one arrow, not an entry on every refresh.

Every entry uses **500× leverage and collateral equal to 10% of current total paper equity**, rounded down to six decimals. Equity is free cash plus the nonnegative estimated settlement payouts of all open paper positions, including unrealized P&L and their saved fee, spread and holding assumptions. Sizing occurs after reversal closes. Cash is never overdrawn: if free cash cannot cover the full 10%, that arrow is skipped with a visible reason. Example with zero costs and unchanged prices: $100 equity → $10 collateral, $90 cash; next green arrow → another $10 collateral, $80 cash. At 500× these are $5,000 notional entries. Fees and price movement change later sizing.

Arrow generation uses the same readiness/direction-change predicate as the plotted arrows: current sample ready, previous sample exists, current signal nonzero and differs from the previous signal. Trading evaluates only the latest completed chart interval, with the previous interval contiguous. An arrow forming on the current candle remains provisional until candle close. Entries execute at a current fresh mark, not the historical candle price. The interval and indicator parameters are captured when Bot starts; changing market, candle interval, indicator parameters, disabling the indicator, switching to Live or resetting the paper wallet stops it. Zoom changes that retain the same interval do not change its rules. Panning does not replay arrows. Start does not trade old arrows, and repeated chart draws cannot trade an arrow twice. Failed orders consume that arrow; the next new arrow can try again.

The paper bot runs while the terminal is visible. Hiding the app pauses arrow processing; reopening the app does not replay a backlog (only the latest completed interval can qualify). Closing/reloading the app leaves Bot OFF; open paper positions persist. Android's existing foreground monitor continues liquidation handling while enabled. Bot ON does not promise background arrow execution.

BTC at 500× follows the existing configured BTC → BTCDEGEN order routing and uses BTCDEGEN's own fresh execution quote; the structural arrows still come from the selected BTC chart. Unsupported markets do not silently reduce leverage. Orders require fresh execution prices and fresh marks for every open paper position so total equity is known; cap changes, missing data or insufficient capital produce a visible skipped-arrow reason. The displayed status names the execution market. The bot never invokes a wallet, signing, approval or live-order path.

Native bot processing synchronizes reversal, equity sizing, exact collateral entry and persistence under the ledger lock. Bot ownership survives process restarts. Android and browser paper implementations include both trading fee sides, adverse entry/exit spread, saved borrow/funding assumptions, and liquidation with losses capped at collateral. These are paper estimates; leverage does not bypass market caps or entry-cost liquidation checks.

Validation: JavaScript strategy tests, native ledger regressions and browser UI tests cover fixed 500×, 10% equity sizing, repeated arrows, stacking, reversal, manual-position ownership, fees, stale quotes, exhausted cash, native liquidation, historical replay protection, button visibility and portrait/landscape layouts. Android release CI also verifies installation, startup, upgrade, process restart, WebView controls and the optimizer worker.


## v1.8.7 — Bot options, hedged tiles and Chandelier profit exits

The Options button immediately beside Bot opens a persistent settings dialog. Defaults: tile both directions ON, Chandelier profit exits ON, 10% of total paper equity per new confirmed arrow, 500× leverage, 14 completed one-minute candles for ATR, 3× ATR distance, and a minimum net profit of 0% of collateral (strictly positive net P&L is always required). Allocation, leverage, ATR period/multiplier and minimum profit are configurable; market leverage caps remain enforced. Save stops new entries, and Bot restarts with a captured settings snapshot. Settings survive app restart. New entries still require the visible terminal; exits on existing Android positions continue whenever the foreground price monitor receives quotes.

With tiling enabled, green arrows add individual long positions and red arrows add individual shorts, even while opposite positions remain open. Neither side forces the other side to close. Each position independently holds until its Chandelier profit exit, a manual close or liquidation. Entries that cannot fund the full allocation from free cash are skipped, without partially sizing or closing existing tiles. The bot remains armed and can add later arrows if a profitable exit releases enough cash. Disabling tiling restores v1.8.6 reversal behavior. Neutral intervals and repeated redraws never add positions.

ATR uses Wilder smoothing of authentic completed one-minute execution-market candles, independent of the arrow chart interval. True range is max(high−low, abs(high−previousClose), abs(low−previousClose)); the first contiguous candle uses high−low. The first period ranges seed a simple mean; subsequent ATR = (previous ATR × (period−1) + current true range) / period. Gaps and placeholder candles reset the ATR warmup. Forming candles are excluded. Zero or missing ATR does not trigger a stop. The retained native execution history is capped at 600 candles; ATR recomputes over the available contiguous history. Starting Bot requests execution-market history to warm exits, and downloaded authentic candles enter the native ledger's candle cache.

Each position tracks the highest and lowest fresh observed execution mark since entry. Long candidate = highest mark − ATR × multiplier; short candidate = lowest mark + ATR × multiplier. The stored long stop can only rise, and the short stop can only fall. This is a per-position Chandelier-style trail using observed marks since entry, not a shared rolling high/low across the whole portfolio. Stops appear as labeled CE lines on the chart, with warming/armed/waiting status in Positions. A position arms when its current estimated net P&L is strictly positive and at least the configured minimum percent of its own collateral. A stop touch closes only if this same net-profit test also passes at the actual current fill mark. Saved opening/closing fees, adverse spread and holding costs are included. A gap can jump beyond a profitable fill: the profit gate then leaves the position open instead of booking a trail-driven loss. Liquidation is checked first and can still realize losses; these exits do not guarantee profitable trades.

The Apply exit settings to existing bot positions checkbox is selected by default. Saving with this checked updates bot-owned open positions, resets their trail/armed state and starts their extrema at the current fresh mark (or their entry if stale). Existing leverage and collateral stay unchanged. Unchecked, existing positions retain their saved exit settings and only new entries use the new configuration. Manual positions are never modified. Older v1.8.6 bot positions have no Chandelier settings until explicitly updated through this dialog. Stopping Bot stops new entries while saved exits remain managed. To disable Chandelier management for existing tiles, uncheck it and save with Apply existing selected, or close positions manually.

Native reversal/tiling, sizing, stop updates and settlement use the synchronized ledger. Every position stores its options, extrema, stop and armed flag. Trail state checkpoints with the normal native book saves (up to five seconds between non-settlement writes); opens, closes and explicit option updates force persistence. Multiple positions sharing an ATR period reuse one ATR calculation per incoming native tick. Browser fallback follows the same profit gates and Wilder formulas while the page receives quotes.

Verification extends the strategy/native regressions with alternating long/short tiles to the cash limit, Wilder ATR values, forming-candle exclusion, gap resets, monotonic long/short stops, fee-aware profit checks, minimum-profit-at-exit checks, settled profit receipts and updating existing positions. Browser QA verifies dialog validation, saved settings, apply-existing behavior, tiling without reversal, adjacent controls and mobile layouts. Android instrumentation verifies the dialog, default hedging/profit settings, settings save and native methods in the signed APK.


## v1.8.8 — Entries on live arrow appearance

This supersedes the candle-close entry timing in v1.8.6/v1.8.7. The bot evaluates the **currently forming chart interval** using the same ready/nonzero/direction-change predicate that paints the live triangle. It opens at the first observed arrow appearance, using a fresh execution mark; it does not wait for that candle to close. Normal renders evaluate the bot without the old 500 ms throttle, and painting the latest live arrow also dispatches bot evaluation in that paint pass. Timing therefore follows delivered quotes and chart updates, rather than a guaranteed wall-clock fill latency.

The live cursor requires an authentic current bucket and contiguous preceding bucket. Closed historical candles, missing current candles, panned historical markers and resume backlogs never create a delayed entry. Starting Bot may immediately act on an eligible arrow already visible on the current candle. For each running session and candle, a long appearance and a short appearance can each execute once. Repeated redraws, disappear/reappear flicker and switching back to an already handled side in the same candle do not duplicate entries. Each side is consumed when its attempt is made, including an order rejected for cash/caps/costs; it is not retried on every refresh. A new candle has a fresh pair of side slots. Stopping/restarting Bot creates a new cursor/session.

An opposite arrow appearing intrabar can add its own tile immediately, subject to cash and settings. In hedged mode it preserves opposite positions; reversal mode retains its configured close-and-reverse behavior. If an intrabar arrow later disappears or changes by candle close, the opened position remains managed by its saved Chandelier/manual/liquidation rules. The bot does not cancel or retroactively erase a paper fill because the signal repainted.

Chandelier profit exits, their completed-one-minute-candle Wilder ATR, saved settings, 10% equity / 500× defaults, native background exit monitoring and foreground-only new entries are unchanged. Tests cover a neutral forming candle becoming green before close, same-candle red appearance, redraw/repaint deduplication, live arrows present on start, new-candle handling, historical/gap rejection, and dispatch from the live arrow painter. Android instrumentation verifies the live cursor inside the actual WebView.


## v1.8.9 — Position return percentages and side totals

Every displayed owned position now shows net P&L in dollars followed by its P&L percent in the same row. Percent = net P&L / that position's collateral × 100. Leverage is already reflected in the net P&L and is not multiplied again. Saved fees, adverse spread and holding estimates remain included in paper net P&L; live positions retain the existing live net-estimate calculation. History rows use their settled net P&L and recorded collateral. Missing net or collateral displays an unavailable value instead of fabricating a percentage. Values show two decimal places, with positive/negative colors and signed gains.

A fixed three-column row directly below the Positions/History/Bot/Options/Close controls shows **Long**, **Short** and **Both** P&L percentages for open positions in the selected chart market and current Paper/Live mode. BTC's chart includes its existing BTCDEGEN execution family. These totals include both manual and bot-owned positions displayed in that market, and exclude pending orders, closed history and other chart markets. The summary remains an open-position summary when the History tab is selected. Its accessibility label and per-group title identify the scope, position counts, net dollars and collateral.

Each group uses sum(net P&L) / sum(collateral) × 100, rather than adding or simply averaging individual percentages. Example: a $10 long earning $5 plus a $90 long earning $9 gives a combined long return of 14%, not an unweighted 30%. A $20 short losing $2 gives Short −10%; Both = ($14−$2)/($100+$20) = +10%. A group with no positions shows 0.00%. If any position in a group has unavailable net P&L or collateral, that group shows an unavailable total; the other complete side can still display normally. Paper positions without any mark/cached reference do not fabricate an entry-price-based return.

The summary occupies a fixed 30-pixel row; the bot status is compacted to a single line with its complete text in the tooltip. Existing panel sizing and draggable limits stay intact, and default phone layouts keep the position amount/percent line visible. Browser checks verify individual and history percentages, unequal collateral weighting, current-market scope, unavailable-data behavior, summary row placement and visible positions at 393×820, 360×640 and 820×393. Android instrumentation checks the bundled percentage math and summary controls. Bot entry timing, tiling and Chandelier exit behavior are unchanged.

Release verification for v1.8.9 retries transient BTC REST failures for up to 30 seconds / 12 attempts in the price and funding probes. The existing source timestamp, mark/index validation and stale/future rejection rules are unchanged. This affects read-only diagnostics only; app pricing and trading logic are unchanged.

## v1.9.0 — dollar exposure and multi-strategy alpha search
The Positions toolbar shows owned open Long and Short dollar exposure immediately after Close, scoped to the selected market and paper/live account. Exposure is collateral × leverage, independent of unrealized P&L; BTC includes its displayed BTCDEGEN positions. A tooltip includes exact dollar totals and percentage weights. Large totals use compact dollar notation. The existing collateral-weighted P&L row remains below the buttons.

The lazy search domain now includes every supported indicator parameter, all nine simulation cost/risk values, and eleven strategy values. Every axis accepts explicit lists or min:max:step ranges (up to 10,001 values per axis); continuous values require a finite step. Supported models: legacy signal/flat, opposite-arrow close-and-reverse, hedge, close profitable opposite tiles while retaining losers, hold until risk/end exit, and opposite-arrow close without immediate reverse. Each can vary adding on/off, long/short/both, total-equity/free-cash sizing, position cap, no additional exit/Chandelier/TP-SL/both, ATR period/multiplier, net minimum profit, take-profit and stop-loss collateral percentages. The huge preset includes all model combinations plus ranges for every numeric dimension. It does not claim all real numbers or arbitrary strategies are exhaustively tested. Budget remains at most one million unique candidates; the exact finite cardinality and sampled/full coverage are displayed.

The multi-position simulator reserves collateral per tile, checks free cash and the tile cap, accounts for leverage, entry/exit slippage, both-side fees, borrow, signed funding, liquidation and cash exhaustion. Adding opens on a new historical arrow rather than every continuing signal sample. Chandelier uses only completed prior-sample ATR and prior trailing extremes, tightens monotonically, and requires net profit strictly above zero and the configured minimum. OHLC adverse liquidation/stop barriers take priority where event ordering is unknown. Profit targets fill at the next open if it already passes a target, otherwise at the estimated barrier. All open tiles settle at data gaps or split end. No cross-split positions are carried. Feature caching includes candidate gap settings.

Historical signals execute at the next sample open; OHLC cannot reconstruct the live bot's intrabar arrow appearances or repaints. The live bot continues to enter on arrow appearance; this release changes research models, not its execution policy. Search selection remains the worse of chronological training and validation scores (ROI − 1.5 × maximum drawdown, at least five trades in each); final 20% holdout is evaluated only for the selected winner. Indicator formulas and indicator compatibility version remain 1.6.0; strategy simulator version is 1.9.0. Applying a winner applies only the indicator and does not change bot/live trade settings.

Reports show the top ten candidates with train/validation ROI, drawdown, trades, wins and fees; expandable full parameters/costs/strategy/metrics; per-family tried/eligible/overflow counts and best/mean validation results; split dates; and a paginated audit of the latest 2,000 individual trials including method, status, score, coordinates, both selection metrics and decoded settings. Family totals cover all evaluations, including trials beyond retention. JSON export includes this audit, range grid, baseline costs, seed, split dates, families and leaders. Mobile renders the detail panel below selected results; wide screens place it alongside. Local-storage pressure falls back to the latest 100 trials for persistence while the full in-memory retained report remains exportable.
